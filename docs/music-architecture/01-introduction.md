This is a consolidated, source-backed reference for producing full-on and morning psytrance, organised so each numbered section can become one agent skill. Numbers are starting points gathered from producer tutorials, not laws; where sources disagree, both positions are kept and flagged in the Open debates section.

Proposed skill split (one skill per row; each maps to a section below):

| Skill | Section | Trigger it should answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| psy-genre-profiler | Genre map | "What makes this full-on vs morning vs twilight?" / target BPM, key, mood |
| psy-kick-designer | Kick design | Design or pick a kick; pitch/amp envelopes; click; length |
| psy-bass-designer | Bass design | Synth patch + MIDI patterns for rolling and full-on basses |
| psy-low-end-locker | Kick–bass lock | Tuning, timing offset, phase, ducking, EQ split, mono |
| psy-groove-programmer | Drums and groove | Hats, snares, percussion layering and rolls |
| psy-melody-writer | Harmony and melody | Scales, keys, hooks, motifs, uplifting sequences |
| psy-lead-designer | Leads and synths | Acid/303, squelch, FM grit, gated leads, arps |
| psy-fx-designer | FX and atmospheres | Zaps, risers, delays, granular atmos, vocals |
| psy-arranger | Arrangement | Bar map, builds, drops, the part after the drop, DJ intro/outro |
| psy-mix-engineer | Mixing | Buses, headroom, width, reverb/delay, saturation |
| psy-mastering | Mastering | Loudness targets, clip/limit chain, true peak |

Conventions used throughout: BPM 145 is the default worked example (1/4 note ≈ 414 ms, 1/8 ≈ 207 ms, 1/16 ≈ 103 ms). Step numbers refer to a 16-step bar (steps 1, 5, 9, 13 are the quarter-note beats).

> Full-on is the melodic, high-energy, peak-time strand of psytrance at roughly 142–150 BPM; "morning" is a mood variant of it (brighter, happier), not a separate tempo. The term "morning psytrance" is ambiguous, so an agent should confirm which of the two below the user means.

| Style | BPM | Low end | Harmony / mood | Reference artists and labels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full-on (Israeli / classic) | 140–150 (most sources 144–148) | Rolling bass, 2–3 short 16ths between kicks, often melodic across octaves | Bright, uplifting, catchy hooks; vocals, guitar, cinematic samples | Astrix, Vibe Tribe, Sesto Sento, Bliss, Ananda Shake; HOMmega, Dacru |
| Morning full-on | \~143–146 | Same engine, lighter and less aggressive | Major or modally ambiguous, euphoric, "old-school" melodic, ethnic/female chants | Digicult, Sonic Species, Melicia, Psydrop, DNA |
| Night / twilight full-on | 145–150 | Bolder, lower bass notes | Darker, twisted synthetic sounds, FM/PM | Ajja, Burn in Noise, Tristan, Mad Tribe, Dickster |
| Morning progressive (a.k.a. "morning psy") | 138–142 (dreamier offshoots 120–128) | Deep, rolling but softer, warmer | Warm melodies, pads, guitars, mantra vocals, tribal perc | Takora and the progressive-psy scene |
| Nitzhonot (ancestor/cousin) | 145–155 | Hard rolling bass, "laserkicks" | Oriental arps, triumphant leads, key changes | — |

Defining full-on traits to enforce:

- **Rolling bass**: two or three short bass notes between each kick, cut off after each kick; Growling Mad Scientists (GMS) are often credited with inventing it.
- **Melodic bass**: unlike a single-note psy bass, full-on bass lines move across notes and octaves to create melody inside the groove.
- **Fast sequence changes**: sequences change quickly — sources cite a maximum of about 32 beats per sequence.
- **Pop-adjacent hooks**: influences from uplifting/nitzhonot, vocal trance and electro house; occasional electric guitar and vocals.
- **Structure**: minimal breakdown with a rising filter sweep, a cinematic sample in near-silence, then the kick and bass slam back.
- **Era**: 2004–2009 is widely called the golden age of full-on, before progressive psy took over the global scene.
